# UK Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) -- Research Document

**Date:** 16 March 2026
**Purpose:** Comprehensive research on UK Lasting Powers of Attorney for implementation in Fynla's Estate Planning module.

---

## 1. Overview

A Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) is a legal document that allows a person (the **donor**) to appoint one or more trusted individuals (the **attorneys**) to make decisions on their behalf. LPAs are governed by the **Mental Capacity Act 2005** and administered by the **Office of the Public Guardian (OPG)**.

LPAs replaced the older Enduring Power of Attorney (EPA) in October 2007. Existing EPAs remain valid but no new EPAs can be created.

---

## 2. Two Types of LPA

### 2.1 Property and Financial Affairs LPA

Covers decisions about the donor's money and property, including:

- Managing bank and building society accounts
- Paying bills and collecting benefits/pensions
- Buying and selling property
- Managing investments and shares
- Managing tax affairs
- Running a business on the donor's behalf

**When attorneys can act:** The donor can choose one of two options:
- **As soon as the LPA is registered** -- attorneys can act while the donor still has mental capacity (with the donor's consent)
- **Only when the donor lacks mental capacity** -- attorneys cannot act until a medical professional or the Court of Protection confirms the donor has lost capacity

The first option is often recommended because it allows attorneys to help with day-to-day finances if the donor becomes physically unwell, is abroad, or simply wants assistance -- without needing to prove loss of capacity.

### 2.2 Health and Welfare LPA

Covers decisions about the donor's health and personal welfare, including:

- Daily routine (washing, dressing, eating)
- Medical care and treatment decisions
- Moving into a care home
- Life-sustaining treatment decisions (if the donor opts in)
- Social services and community care
- Who the donor has contact with

**When attorneys can act:** Health and Welfare attorneys can **ONLY** act when the donor **lacks mental capacity** to make the specific decision in question. This is a fundamental legal restriction -- there is no option for attorneys to act while the donor retains capacity. Each decision is assessed independently; the donor may lack capacity for some decisions but retain it for others.

### 2.3 Life-Sustaining Treatment Decisions

The Health and Welfare LPA includes a critical section (Section 7) where the donor must make an explicit choice:

- **Option A:** The donor gives their attorneys authority to consent to or refuse life-sustaining treatment on their behalf
- **Option B:** The donor does NOT give their attorneys this authority

If Option B is selected, decisions about life-sustaining treatment will be made by the treating medical team in the donor's best interests. This is one of the most significant decisions in the LPA process and cannot be changed after registration without creating a new LPA.

## 3. OPG Form Structure and Sections

Each LPA type has its own form (LP1F for Property & Financial Affairs, LP1H for Health & Welfare). The forms share a similar structure:

### Section 1 -- The Donor
- Full legal name
- Date of birth
- Address

### Section 2 -- The Attorneys
- Full legal names of each attorney
- Dates of birth
- Addresses
- **Replacement attorneys** (backup attorneys who step in if an original attorney can no longer act)

### Section 3 -- How Attorneys Should Act
When multiple attorneys are appointed, the donor chooses:
- **Jointly** -- all attorneys must agree on every decision (if one dies or can no longer act, none of them can act)
- **Jointly and severally** -- attorneys can act together or independently (most flexible)
- **Jointly for some decisions, jointly and severally for others** -- a hybrid approach (the donor must specify which decisions require joint action)

### Section 4 -- Restrictions and Conditions (Property & Financial Affairs)
The donor can add specific restrictions (attorneys MUST follow) or conditions (circumstances that must be met before attorneys can act). Examples:
- Attorneys must keep accounts and financial records
- Attorneys must not sell the donor's home without specific consent
- A specific attorney handles investment decisions while another handles day-to-day banking

### Section 5 -- When the LPA Can Be Used (Property & Financial Affairs only)
- While the donor has capacity AND when they lack capacity
- Only when the donor lacks capacity
(Health & Welfare LPAs can only ever be used when the donor lacks capacity)

### Section 6 -- Preferences
Non-binding wishes the donor would like attorneys to consider. These guide attorneys but are not legally enforceable.

### Section 7 -- Life-Sustaining Treatment (Health & Welfare only)
The explicit Option A / Option B choice described above.

### Section 8 -- The Certificate Provider
A person who confirms that:
- The donor understands the purpose and scope of the LPA
- No fraud or undue pressure is being used
- There is nothing that would prevent the LPA from being created

### Section 9 -- People to Notify
People who should be told when the LPA is registered, giving them the opportunity to object.

### Section 10 -- Donor Signature
The donor signs the LPA (or makes a mark if unable to sign, with a witness).

### Section 11 -- Attorney Signatures
Each attorney and replacement attorney signs to confirm they accept the appointment.

### Section 12 -- Application to Register
The registration section submitted to the OPG.

## 4. Key Legal Requirements

### 4.1 Donor Requirements
- Must be **aged 18 or over**
- Must have **mental capacity** at the time the LPA is created (not at registration, but at signing)
- Must sign the LPA (or direct someone to sign on their behalf if physically unable)

### 4.2 Attorney Requirements
- Must be **aged 18 or over**
- Must have **mental capacity**
- Must not be **bankrupt** or subject to a debt relief order (for Property & Financial Affairs LPA -- bankrupt individuals CAN still be Health & Welfare attorneys)
- Can be a family member, friend, spouse, partner, or professional (e.g. solicitor)
- Cannot be the donor's care home owner/manager if the donor lives in a care home (for Health & Welfare)

### 4.3 Certificate Provider Requirements
The certificate provider is a crucial safeguard. They must be ONE of:
- **A person who has known the donor personally for at least 2 years** ("knowledge" certificate provider)
- **A professional** -- a registered healthcare professional, solicitor, barrister, social worker, Independent Mental Capacity Advocate, or other professional with relevant expertise

The certificate provider:
- Must be aged 18 or over
- Cannot be a family member of the donor or any attorney
- Cannot be a business partner of the donor or any attorney
- Cannot be an employee of the donor or any attorney
- Cannot be an attorney or replacement attorney named in the LPA
- Cannot be an owner, director, manager, or employee of a care home where the donor lives

**The 2-year rule:** If the certificate provider is a "knowledge" provider (not a professional), they must have known the donor personally for at least 2 years. This is a strict requirement -- professional or casual acquaintance does not count.

### 4.4 People to Notify (Named Persons)
- The donor can name up to **5 people** to be notified when the LPA is registered
- These people are NOT attorneys -- they are independent persons who can raise objections
- They receive formal notification from the OPG before registration is completed
- They have 3 weeks to raise an objection
- Named persons cannot be attorneys or replacement attorneys in the same LPA
- If no named persons are listed, a second certificate provider is NOT required (this changed from the original rules)

### 4.5 Signature Requirements
- The donor's signature must be a **wet ink signature** (or a mark witnessed by two people if the donor cannot sign)
- Each attorney must sign with a wet ink signature
- The certificate provider must sign with a wet ink signature
- All signatures must be witnessed by an independent person aged 18+
- The witness cannot be an attorney named in the LPA
- **Digital/electronic signatures are not accepted** by the OPG
- Signing must happen in the correct order: donor first, then certificate provider, then attorneys

### 4.6 Execution Order
The LPA must be signed in a specific order:
1. The **donor** signs first (Section 10)
2. The **certificate provider** signs (Section 8) -- must be signed on the same day as or after the donor
3. Each **attorney** signs (Section 11) -- must be signed on the same day as or after the certificate provider
4. **People to notify** are informed (before or during registration)

If this order is not followed, the OPG will reject the LPA.

## 5. Registration Process

### 5.1 When to Register
An LPA must be **registered with the Office of the Public Guardian** before it can be used. Registration can happen:
- Immediately after the LPA is signed (recommended)
- At any point before the donor loses capacity
- An unregistered LPA has no legal force

### 5.2 How to Register
1. Complete the registration section of the LPA form
2. Pay the registration fee
3. Send the completed LPA form to the OPG
4. The OPG notifies any named persons
5. Named persons have 3 weeks to raise objections
6. If no objections, the OPG registers the LPA and returns the original document

### 5.3 Fees
- **Registration fee: currently GBP 82 per LPA** (so GBP 164 for both types)
- Fee reductions/exemptions are available for people receiving certain means-tested benefits:
  - **Full exemption (GBP 0):** If the donor receives Universal Credit, Income Support, Income-based Jobseeker's Allowance, Income-related Employment and Support Allowance, or has an income below GBP 12,000
  - **50% reduction (GBP 41):** If the donor has an income below GBP 18,000 (after tax)
- Fees are non-refundable unless the OPG rejects the LPA due to their error

### 5.4 Registration Timeline
- Standard processing: **8 to 10 weeks** from submission
- If objections are raised, this can take significantly longer
- The OPG may return the LPA for corrections, which adds further time
- Once registered, the LPA is valid indefinitely (unless revoked or the donor dies)

### 5.5 Online Registration
Since 2024, the OPG has offered an online service ("Make a lasting power of attorney") at https://www.lastingpowerofattorney.service.gov.uk/. This allows:
- Creating and completing the LPA digitally
- Signing and witnessing still require wet ink (printed pages)
- Paying the fee online
- Tracking the registration status

## 6. What Happens Without LPAs

### 6.1 If No LPA Exists and the Person Loses Capacity

Without an LPA, **nobody** has automatic legal authority to manage another person's finances or make health decisions on their behalf -- not even a spouse, partner, or adult child.

### 6.2 Court of Protection

If someone loses mental capacity without an LPA in place, family members must apply to the **Court of Protection** for a **deputyship order**. This involves:

- **Application fee: GBP 371** (Court of Protection application)
- **Annual supervision fee: GBP 320 per year** for ongoing oversight by the OPG
- **Assessment fee: potentially GBP 100+** for a capacity assessment
- **Legal costs: GBP 1,000 to GBP 3,000+** for solicitor assistance (often necessary due to complexity)
- **Total first-year cost: typically GBP 1,500 to GBP 4,000+** vs GBP 82 for an LPA

### 6.3 Practical Consequences
- **Processing time:** Deputyship applications typically take **4 to 6 months** (vs 8-10 weeks for LPA registration)
- **No choice in deputy:** The court decides who becomes deputy, which may not be the person the individual would have chosen
- **Ongoing supervision:** Deputies must submit annual reports to the OPG and may need to provide a security bond
- **Restricted authority:** Deputies often have more limited powers than LPA attorneys
- **Family disputes:** Without clear instructions, family disagreements about care and finances can escalate, sometimes requiring further court involvement
- **Frozen accounts:** Banks and financial institutions may freeze accounts while the deputyship application is processed, causing immediate financial hardship
- **Health decisions:** Without a Health & Welfare LPA, medical decisions are made by healthcare professionals in the patient's "best interests" -- family views are considered but not binding

### 6.4 Financial Planning Implications
- Access to savings, investments, and pensions may be blocked for months
- Mortgage payments, insurance premiums, and bills may go unpaid
- Investment portfolios cannot be managed or rebalanced
- Property cannot be sold (e.g. to fund care home fees)
- Tax returns cannot be filed
- Pension drawdown decisions cannot be made

## 7. Compliance Rules for the Fynla Application

### 7.1 What Fynla CAN Do
- **Record-keeping:** Store details about existing LPAs (type, attorneys, registration status, review dates)
- **Reminders:** Prompt users to review their LPAs periodically
- **Gap analysis:** Identify whether users have both LPA types in place
- **Educational content:** Explain what LPAs are, why they matter, and the process of creating one
- **Cost information:** Display current OPG fees
- **Checklists:** Help users prepare the information they need before visiting a solicitor
- **Document storage:** Allow users to upload scanned copies of their registered LPAs for reference
- **Attorney contact details:** Store attorney names and contact information for emergency reference
- **Review tracking:** Track when LPAs were last reviewed and suggest periodic reviews

### 7.2 What Fynla MUST NOT Do
- **Generate legal documents:** Fynla must not create, draft, or produce LPA forms -- this is a regulated legal activity
- **Provide legal advice:** Fynla must not advise users on specific LPA decisions (e.g. who to appoint as attorney, what restrictions to include)
- **Act as a certificate provider:** The application cannot verify capacity or certify LPA documents
- **Facilitate signing:** The application must not present itself as part of the legal signing/execution process
- **Replace solicitor consultation:** All guidance must include clear disclaimers that professional legal advice should be sought

### 7.3 Mandatory Disclaimers
Every LPA-related screen must include a disclaimer along the lines of:
> "This tool is for planning and record-keeping purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Always consult a qualified solicitor when creating or updating a Lasting Power of Attorney."

This mirrors the existing disclaimer pattern used in the Will Planning tab.

### 7.4 Data Sensitivity
LPA data is particularly sensitive because it contains:
- Names and contact details of attorneys (trusted individuals)
- Health and welfare preferences
- Life-sustaining treatment wishes
- Financial management arrangements

All LPA data must be treated with the same level of security as other financial data in the application, subject to GDPR (especially health-related data under Article 9 special categories).

## 8. LPA Statistics and Context

- The OPG registers approximately **900,000 LPAs per year** in England and Wales
- An estimated **55% of UK adults** do not have an LPA in place
- The number of LPA registrations has increased year-on-year, driven by an ageing population and greater awareness
- LPAs are particularly relevant for:
  - Couples approaching or in retirement
  - Individuals with significant assets or complex financial arrangements
  - People with dependants who rely on their income
  - Business owners who need continuity arrangements
  - Anyone with specific healthcare wishes

## 10. Key Terminology Reference

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Donor | The person creating the LPA |
| Attorney | The person(s) appointed to act on the donor's behalf |
| Replacement attorney | A backup attorney who steps in if an original attorney cannot act |
| Certificate provider | The independent person who certifies the donor understands the LPA |
| Named person | A person notified when the LPA is registered, who can raise objections |
| OPG | Office of the Public Guardian -- the government body that registers and supervises LPAs |
| Court of Protection | The court that makes decisions for people who lack mental capacity |
| Deputy | A person appointed by the Court of Protection (when no LPA exists) |
| Mental capacity | The ability to make a specific decision at a specific time |
| Best interests | The legal standard used when making decisions for someone who lacks capacity |
| Wet ink signature | A physical signature made with pen on paper (not electronic) |
| Jointly | All attorneys must agree on every decision |
| Jointly and severally | Attorneys can act together or independently |
