## What each walk proved

**Household (i):**
- **Pension relief (higher rate):** "Pay £9,400 more into your pension and save £3,760 in tax".
  - It is sized after the £3,000 already paid in.
  - The slice includes £2,700 of savings interest, which also sits in the 40% band.
  - Without the fix it would have said £12,400. The walk was taken after review fix `ecd0945f9`.
- **Salary sacrifice now fires** for the form-captured workplace pension (B1): £60, which is 2% National Insurance above the upper earnings limit on £3,000.
- **Lifetime ISA:** it is listed, carries no "saves" chip, and is not in the total.

**Household (ii):**
- **Double counting (B4):** ISA top-up (£180), gift-to-spouse (£1,150) and joint split (£675) all shelter the same interest. Only the gift counts toward the total. The other two carry `counted_in_total: false` and the note `Alternative to "Gift £150,000 of savings to your spouse …" — compare before doing both.` (read server-side from `ComposedTaxPlanService`).
- **Marriage Allowance and the gift:** Marriage Allowance (£252) fires, and the gift's capacity shows the spouse's allowance reduced by the transferred £1,260 (£17,310 = £11,310 + £5,000 + £1,000).

**Household (iii):**
- **B7 fixed:** Marriage Allowance (£252) now appears for a dual-earner couple whose spouse earns £8,000, below the Personal Allowance less £1,260, so the transferring spouse pays no extra tax.
- **Modest-earner spouse pension:** £1,600.
- **Basic-rate pension relief:** £700, which is 10% of earnings.

**Public funnel (ruling b):** the funnel asks "Do you have a spouse or civil partner?" and "What is your spouse or civil partner's annual income?"
