A gift to another person is a potentially exempt transfer. If the giver lives
seven years after making it, it is exempt (Inheritance Tax Act 1984 s3A(4)).
If the giver dies within seven years, it becomes chargeable.

Gifts that become chargeable are counted against the nil rate band first, in
the order they were made. Whatever band they use is no longer there for the
estate, so the estate itself pays more tax. Tax is due on a gift itself only
where the chargeable gifts made in those seven years come to more than the nil
rate band.

Taper relief (s7(4)) reduces the tax charged on a gift made more than three
years before death, in steps for each further year up to the seventh. It
reduces the tax on the gift, not the gift's value, so it only matters where
tax is due on the gift itself. GOV.UK says the same: taper relief only applies
if the gifts made in the seven years before death come to more than the
tax-free threshold.

Who pays: the person who received the gift is liable for the tax on it
(s199(1)(b)). The giver's personal representatives are liable for it only if it
is still unpaid twelve months after the end of the month in which the giver
died (s204(8)). GOV.UK describes Inheritance Tax on gifts as usually paid by
the estate; under the Act the recipient is liable first, and the estate pays
the extra tax caused by the gifts using up the band.

get_tax_information (inheritance_tax), never from memory. Name the section
when stating who pays or how taper relief works.
