## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

ISAs are individual accounts: there is no such thing as a joint ISA under UK
law, so a couple's tax-free wrapper capacity is two entirely separate
allowances, one in each partner's own name, each resetting every tax year.
When one partner has filled their own allowance and the other has unused
capacity, surplus household savings can still be sheltered — by subscribing
to the other partner's own ISA rather than leaving the money taxed outside a
wrapper. The strategy applies to dual-earner couples where the user's
subscriptions have reached this year's allowance and the spouse's have not;
it is for households saving more than one allowance can hold, and it has
nothing to add while the user's own allowance still has room.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla fires this recommendation only when the user's recorded ISA
subscriptions have actually reached the annual allowance and the spouse's
recorded position shows headroom — it never assumes either side. What it
quantifies is the spouse's remaining capacity for the year: the amount of
additional household money that could be sheltered now rather than waiting
for the user's allowance to reset in April. Fynla does not put a tax-saved
figure on the headline, because the saving depends on what the money would
otherwise earn; the capacity itself is the fact worth surfacing.

## Where it sits in sequence

By construction this strategy comes after the user's own ISA moves — it only
exists once the user's own allowance is full, so wrapping one's own cash and
investments always happens first. It conflicts with nothing and pairs
naturally with the spouse-transfer strategies: money or holdings moved into
the spouse's name can then be wrapped using the spouse's own allowance.

## Claim tier and voicing

Mechanical tier: whether each allowance is full and how much capacity remains
follow directly from recorded subscriptions and the published allowance, so
Fyn states the position plainly. Voicing is careful about ownership: money
subscribed to the spouse's ISA is in the spouse's account and belongs to the
spouse — Fyn says "your spouse's own ISA", never anything that could imply
the account is shared.
