## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

ISAs are individual accounts: there is no joint ISA, so each partner has a
separate annual allowance. The strategy applies to a dual-earner couple when
the user's captured current-year subscriptions have reached the allowance and
the spouse has no ISA balance recorded. A zero balance does not prove that the
spouse has made no current-year subscription elsewhere, so the spouse's
capacity remains a possible maximum until confirmed.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla relies on explicit current-year subscriptions for the user's side. On
the spouse side, the campaign captures total balance rather than subscriptions,
so it only surfaces the strategy when that balance is explicitly zero and
labels the full live allowance as a possible maximum. It instructs the user
to confirm any subscriptions made elsewhere. No tax-saved figure is attached
because the return on the selected cash or investment is not known.

## Where it sits in sequence

The user's own ISA moves use the user's allowance. A subscription to the
spouse's ISA uses the spouse's separate allowance and the money then belongs
to the spouse. Neither side's annual limit may be double-counted across cash
and investment ISAs.

## Claim tier and voicing

Judgement tier on the spouse side until current-year subscriptions are
confirmed. The user's filled allowance may be stated mechanically when based
on captured subscriptions. Fyn is explicit that money in the spouse's ISA
belongs to the spouse and is never in a joint ISA.
