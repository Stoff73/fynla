## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

A Lifetime ISA adds a government bonus of one pound for every four pounds
contributed, on contributions up to its own annual limit. The account can be
opened by adults from eighteen up to the published maximum opening age, and
contributions keep earning the bonus until the age the rules set. The money
is meant for two purposes: buying a first home up to a purchase price cap, or
funding later life from age sixty — withdrawals for anything else carry a
penalty that claws back more than the bonus. The strategy applies to users
young enough to open one who still have ISA allowance available this year;
it suits aspiring first-time buyers best, and long-horizon savers
comfortable leaving the money untouched until sixty.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla sizes the suggested contribution as the smaller of the Lifetime ISA's
own annual limit and the user's remaining overall ISA allowance — Lifetime
ISA contributions count toward the one overall allowance, so the suggestion
never double-counts wrapper capacity. The figure quoted is the annual
government bonus on that contribution at the published bonus rate: free money
that does not depend on investment performance, only on making the
contribution. Eligibility is checked against the user's recorded date of
birth, and the strategy simply does not appear once the opening-age window
has closed.

## Where it sits in sequence

The contribution draws on the same single overall ISA allowance as wrapping
excess cash and bed-and-ISA moves; each strategy is sized against the full
remaining allowance, so Fyn presents them as alternative claims on the same
capacity and the user decides which comes first. The
bonus rate comfortably beats ordinary interest on the same money, but the
penalty regime cuts the other way, so the Lifetime ISA slice is sized for
money genuinely destined for a first home or later life rather than for the
emergency fund.

## Claim tier and voicing

Judgement tier: the bonus arithmetic is simple, but whether the account suits
the user depends on their plans — a first-home purchase inside the price cap,
or patience until sixty — and the withdrawal penalty makes a wrong guess
expensive. Fyn therefore hedges, framing the account as something the user
may want to consider, and always pairs the bonus with the penalty so the
trade is visible before any money is committed.
