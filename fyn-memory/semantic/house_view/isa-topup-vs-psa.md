## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

Interest on ordinary savings is tax-free only up to the Personal Savings
Allowance for the saver's tax band; everything above it is taxed at their
marginal rate, year after year. Moving cash inside an ISA removes that
interest from tax permanently. The strategy applies when someone holds
non-ISA cash whose interest genuinely exceeds their allowance and they still
have ISA allowance left this tax year to absorb it. It is most valuable to
cash-heavy savers in the higher band, whose allowance is smaller, and in the
additional band, where there is no allowance at all; it has nothing to offer
while interest sits comfortably inside the allowance, because there is no tax
to save.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla estimates annual interest from the user's recorded balances and
interest rates rather than assuming a market rate, and compares it with the
allowance for the user's band from the live tax configuration. Only the
excess above the allowance carries tax, so Fynla works out which portion of
the balance is producing taxed interest and recommends wrapping the smallest
of three amounts: the remaining ISA allowance, the total non-ISA cash, and
the slice of balance generating the excess. The saving is that transfer
multiplied by the user's own average rate and marginal tax rate — a recurring
annual saving, because the interest stays sheltered for good. Trivial
transfers are skipped; the recommendation only fires when the move is worth
making.

## Where it sits in sequence

Wrapping the user's own cash comes before moving savings into a spouse's
name: the catalogue sequences this strategy ahead of the savings-to-spouse
transfer, because using one's own ISA allowance keeps both the money and the
tax shelter in the user's hands. That ordering is presentation rather than
arithmetic, though — the spouse gift is computed on the user's full recorded
sole savings, not net of what the wrap takes, so a user following both
should treat the two figures as drawing on the same pot. The strategy also
shares the single overall ISA allowance with the bed-and-ISA investment move
and any Lifetime ISA contribution: each is sized against the full remaining
allowance, so Fyn presents them as alternative uses of the same capacity,
and the user chooses which claims it first.

## Claim tier and voicing

Mechanical tier: the excess interest, the transferable amount, and the saving
all follow from recorded balances, recorded rates, and the published
allowance, so Fyn states the working directly — how much interest the cash
earns, where the allowance runs out, and what wrapping the excess saves each
year. Voicing makes the permanence explicit: this is not a one-off saving but
tax removed from every future year the cash stays wrapped.
