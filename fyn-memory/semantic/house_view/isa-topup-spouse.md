## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

Every eligible adult has their own ISA allowance each tax year, whether or not
they earn. ISAs are individual accounts; there is no joint ISA. The campaign
can surface this route only when the spouse has explicitly recorded no
existing ISA balance. That still does not prove current-year headroom because
the spouse may have subscribed to an ISA elsewhere. Fyn therefore presents
the published annual allowance as a possible maximum to confirm before any
subscription. Money gifted and subscribed belongs to the spouse.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla does not subtract total ISA balance from the annual allowance because a
balance can contain subscriptions from many earlier tax years and cannot
establish current-year use. When the recorded balance is zero it may show up
to the full live allowance, with an explicit instruction to confirm
subscriptions made elsewhere. It does not attach a tax-saved headline because
the saving depends on the selected asset and its return. A gift between
eligible spouses or civil partners usually creates no immediate Capital Gains
Tax charge and may qualify for the Inheritance Tax spouse exemption, but the
money changes beneficial ownership and the relevant conditions must be met.

## Where it sits in sequence

This is the single-earner counterpart of ISA coordination for a dual-earner
couple. The spouse's allowance is separate from the user's, but every
subscription in the spouse's name draws on the spouse's one annual ISA limit.

## Claim tier and voicing

Judgement tier until current-year subscriptions are confirmed. Fyn may state
the published maximum but must not call it confirmed headroom. Voicing is
exact about ownership: the account and money belong to the spouse and are
never described as shared.
