## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

Investments held outside an ISA build up unrealised capital gains that will
be taxed when eventually sold. A bed-and-ISA move sells holdings in the
taxable account and immediately rebuys them inside the ISA: the sale
crystallises gains, and gains within the Capital Gains Tax annual exempt
amount are tax-free, so repeating the exercise each year walks money into the
wrapper without ever paying tax on the way in. Once inside, all future
growth, dividends, and interest are sheltered for good. The strategy applies
to anyone with unrealised gains in non-ISA holdings and remaining ISA
allowance this tax year; it suits long-term investors who would otherwise let
gains compound into a future tax bill, and it has nothing to work with when
holdings stand at a loss or the ISA allowance is already spent.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla totals the unrealised gains across the user's non-ISA holdings from
their recorded cost basis and current values, then caps the gains worth
realising at the annual exempt amount — realising more would trigger tax,
which defeats the point. The proceeds to move are sized so they fit within
the remaining ISA allowance, scaled to the proportion of gain being
harvested. The saving quoted is the realisable gain multiplied by the
Capital Gains Tax rate for the user's band: the tax a future sale of those
holdings would otherwise have cost, plus the unquantified benefit of
sheltering everything the holdings earn from then on.

## Where it sits in sequence

Bed and ISA draws on the same single overall ISA allowance as wrapping excess
cash and any Lifetime ISA contribution, and Fynla allocates that one pool by
value: whichever strategy saves the most each year takes what it needs
first, each further strategy is re-sized by its own arithmetic on the
allowance that remains, and a strategy the remainder cannot support is
still shown but flagged as crowded out — naming what used the allowance —
and left out of the combined saving. A user with taxed savings interest and
unrealised gains may therefore see this move sized down, or set aside for
the year, when wrapping cash earns the stronger claim. The annual
exempt amount and the ISA allowance both reset each tax year, which is why
Fynla treats this as a yearly housekeeping habit rather than a one-off
rescue.

## Claim tier and voicing

Judgement tier: the arithmetic of the gain and the exempt amount is solid,
but the move involves real choices — which holdings to sell, dealing costs on
the way out and back in, and a short spell out of the market while the trade
settles. Fyn therefore hedges: this is something the user may want to
consider rather than a working to state as settled fact, and the voicing
names the frictions alongside the saving so the decision is genuinely the
user's.
