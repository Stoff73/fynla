## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

Investments held outside an ISA can build up unrealised gains. A bed-and-ISA
move sells an eligible holding and repurchases it inside an ISA. The sale
crystallises a gain. Whether that gain is tax-free depends on the user's net
gains after all other disposals, losses and reliefs for the tax year, not on
this transaction in isolation. Once repurchased inside the ISA, later growth
and income are sheltered while the investment remains in the wrapper. The
strategy requires a non-ISA holding, an unrealised gain and remaining ISA
subscription capacity.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla uses the recorded cost basis and current value of eligible non-ISA
holdings, then limits the illustrated gain by the live annual exempt amount
and the proceeds by remaining ISA capacity. That illustration is conditional:
Fynla does not capture every disposal, allowable loss or relief elsewhere, so
the user must confirm their whole tax-year Capital Gains Tax position before
relying on the exempt amount. The quoted saving is an estimate of tax that
might otherwise arise on that illustrated gain, not a guaranteed cash saving.

## Where it sits in sequence

Bed and ISA draws on the same single overall ISA allowance as wrapping excess
cash and any Lifetime ISA contribution. Fynla allocates that pool across the
eligible strategies; a move may be reduced or crowded out when another use of
the same allowance has a stronger supported saving. The annual exempt amount
and ISA allowance reset each tax year, but neither may be double-counted.

## Claim tier and voicing

Judgement tier: the recorded holding arithmetic is mechanical, but the user's
remaining exempt amount is not known from the captured data. The move also
involves which holdings to sell, ISA subscription limits, dealing costs and
market movement while trades settle. Fyn presents it as a conditional
illustration that needs the full gains-and-losses position checked before
action.
