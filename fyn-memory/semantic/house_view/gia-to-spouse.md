## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

When one partner earns and the other does not, investments held outside an
ISA in the earner's name have their dividends and gains taxed at the earner's
rates, while the non-earning spouse's allowances sit idle. Transferring
General Investment Account holdings to the spouse puts their unused Capital
Gains Tax annual exempt amount and their own Dividend Allowance to work —
both absorb returns tax-free — and anything above the allowances is then
taxed at the spouse's basic rate rather than the earner's higher one.
Transfers between spouses and civil partners are exempt from Capital Gains
Tax and Inheritance Tax, so the holdings can move without triggering a bill.
The strategy applies to single-earner couples where the earning partner
holds investments outside an ISA; it matters most when those holdings pay
meaningful dividends.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla's headline counts the recurring rate difference on the user's recorded
dividend income above the spouse's own Dividend Allowance — the slice that
would still be taxed in the spouse's hands, just at the basic dividend rate
instead of the user's. That is a deliberately conservative figure: the slice
the spouse's allowance absorbs entirely, and the shelter the spouse's annual
exempt amount gives future capital gains, are described as further benefits
rather than counted, because they depend on the size and timing of future
returns. The user's dividend rate is judged on total taxable income, not
salary alone, so a modest earner with a large dividend stream is priced at
the rate they genuinely pay.

## Where it sits in sequence

This is the single-earner counterpart of the General Investment Account
rebalance recommended to dual-earner couples — one or the other fires for a
given household, never both. It has no catalogue ordering constraints or
conflicts, and it composes naturally with the spouse's ISA strategy: holdings
moved into the spouse's name can be wrapped into the spouse's own ISA
allowance year by year, converting a rate improvement into full shelter.

## Claim tier and voicing

Judgement tier: the rate arithmetic is clear, but the move is a genuine,
unconditional gift — ownership and control of the investments pass to the
spouse for good, and whether and how far to go depends on the couple's
circumstances. Fyn therefore hedges, presenting the transfer as something the
couple may want to consider, naming both the conservative quantified saving
and the unquantified allowance benefits, and being candid that the tax
exemption on the transfer relies on the gift being real.
