## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

Non-ISA investments in an earner's name may produce dividends and gains taxed
at that person's rates. A genuine transfer of eligible General Investment
Account holdings to a non-earning spouse may use the spouse's tax position
instead. Fynla cannot assume the spouse's Dividend Allowance or Capital Gains
Tax annual exempt amount is unused because the campaign does not capture every
dividend, disposal, loss or relief. A transfer between eligible spouses or
civil partners is usually made on a no-gain, no-loss basis for Capital Gains
Tax, so there is normally no immediate charge; the recipient inherits the
transferor's base cost and may pay tax on a later disposal. The strategy
applies only when the user has an actual non-ISA investment account.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla does not quote an exact tax saving. User-level dividend income is not
isolated to the holdings that could be transferred, and the spouse's unused
allowances are not known. It can show the live allowance limits as matters to
check, but the saving depends on the selected holdings, the income they
produce, the spouse's complete tax position and the future disposal value.

## Where it sits in sequence

This is the single-earner counterpart of the General Investment Account
rebalance for dual-earner couples. It can coordinate with the spouse's own
ISA, but a later ISA subscription must stay within the spouse's confirmed
current-year capacity.

## Claim tier and voicing

Judgement tier: the move is a genuine, unconditional gift, so ownership and
control pass to the spouse. Fyn presents possible allowance and rate benefits
without a fabricated saving and states that the full tax position, base cost
and ownership consequences need checking.
