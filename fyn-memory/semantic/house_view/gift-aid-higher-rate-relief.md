## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

When someone donates through Gift Aid, the charity reclaims basic-rate tax on
the grossed-up donation — that part happens automatically and benefits the
charity. What many donors miss is the personal side: a higher or
additional-rate taxpayer can reclaim the difference between their own rate
and the basic rate on the grossed-up donation through Self Assessment. The
strategy applies to anyone who has recorded regular charitable giving and
whose total taxable income puts them in the higher or additional band. It has
nothing to add for basic-rate taxpayers, because the charity's reclaim is the
whole of the relief at that band.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla computes the reclaim from the user's recorded annual donations at the
reclaim factor for their band, with a larger factor for additional-rate
taxpayers, reflecting the wider gap between their rate and the basic rate on
the grossed-up amount. The band is judged on total taxable income — including
dividends and savings interest, not employment alone — because that is how the
band is actually determined. Fynla states only the personal reclaim: the
charity's basic-rate reclaim already happens without the user doing anything,
so adding it to the headline would overstate what filing a Self Assessment
return actually returns to the user.

## Where it sits in sequence

This strategy stands alone in the composed plan. It asks for no new money —
the donations have already been made — so it never competes with pension
contributions or ISA moves for the same surplus, and it has no ordering
dependencies or conflicts. It is simply unclaimed tax relief on giving that
has already happened, which is why Fynla surfaces it whenever the recorded
giving and the tax band line up.

## Claim tier and voicing

Mechanical tier: the reclaim follows from recorded donations, the user's
band, and the published rates, so Fyn states the working directly — the
annual giving, the band, and the amount reclaimable through Self Assessment.
Voicing is precise about the mechanism: the user claims this themselves on
their return, the charity's reclaim is separate and unaffected, and the
relief repeats every year the giving continues.
