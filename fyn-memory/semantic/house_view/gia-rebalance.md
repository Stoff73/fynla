## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

When both partners in a couple earn but sit in different tax bands, dividends
from investments held in the higher earner's name are taxed at that partner's
higher dividend rate, while the same holdings in the basic-rate partner's
name would be taxed at the basic dividend rate. Rebalancing General
Investment Account holdings towards the lower-band partner moves the
recurring dividend income to the cheaper rate, and capital gains on an
eventual sale are taxed more gently too. Transfers between spouses and civil
partners are exempt from Capital Gains Tax and Inheritance Tax, which is what
makes the rebalance possible without a tax cost on the way. The strategy
applies to dual-earner couples where the user pays above the basic rate and
the spouse pays the basic rate; where no dividend income is recorded yet,
the suggestion takes its general form — hold taxable investments in the
spouse's name — with no figure attached.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla values the rebalance as the user's recorded dividend income multiplied
by the gap between the dividend rate for their band and the basic dividend
rate — the recurring annual difference that moving the income stream makes.
The user's band is judged on total taxable income, including dividends and
interest, not salary alone, because someone with a modest salary and a large
dividend stream is genuinely a higher-rate payer for this purpose. Fynla
quantifies the dividend saving because it repeats every year; the capital
gains advantage is real but depends on a future sale, so it is described
rather than counted.

## Where it sits in sequence

This is the dual-earner sibling of the General Investment Account transfer
recommended to single-earner couples — only one of the two fires for a given
household, depending on whether the spouse earns. Within the plan it stands
on its own, with no ordering constraints or conflicts; it coordinates
naturally with ISA coordination, since holdings moved to the spouse can in
time be wrapped in the spouse's own ISA allowance.

## Claim tier and voicing

Judgement tier: the rate arithmetic is straightforward, but the move itself
is not — a transfer between spouses must be a genuine, unconditional gift,
ownership and control really do change hands, and the right split depends on
the couple's circumstances and intentions. Fyn therefore hedges, framing the
rebalance as something the couple may want to consider, and is explicit that
the saving rests on the holdings genuinely belonging to the receiving
partner from then on.
