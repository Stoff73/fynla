## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

When partners sit in different tax bands, eligible non-ISA investments in the
higher earner's name may produce dividends taxed at a higher rate than the
same holdings in the lower earner's name. Rebalancing may therefore reduce
tax. A transfer between eligible spouses or civil partners is usually made on
a no-gain, no-loss basis for Capital Gains Tax, with the recipient inheriting
the original base cost. It must be a genuine transfer of ownership. The
strategy applies only when the user has an actual non-ISA investment account
and the recorded user and spouse income positions indicate different bands.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla does not value the rebalance with the user's total recorded dividends,
because those dividends are not linked to the specific transferable holdings.
It also does not know every item in the spouse's tax position. The recorded
income and dividend figures can identify a possible band difference, but the
actual saving depends on which holdings move, the income they produce, the
spouse's other income and the later disposal. No exact saving is stated.

## Where it sits in sequence

This is the dual-earner sibling of the General Investment Account transfer
for a single-earner couple. It can coordinate with ISA use, but subscriptions
must remain within each partner's separately confirmed annual limit.

## Claim tier and voicing

Judgement tier: a transfer must be a genuine, unconditional gift, ownership
and control change hands, and the right split depends on the holdings and the
couple's circumstances. Fyn presents the possible direction without false
precision and calls for the base cost and full tax positions to be checked.
