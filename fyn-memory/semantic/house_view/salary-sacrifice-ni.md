## What this is, when it applies, who it is for

An employee paying into a workplace Defined Contribution pension from their
salary pays National Insurance on that salary before the contribution goes
in. Under salary sacrifice the employee formally gives up part of their
salary and the employer pays it into the pension instead; the sacrificed
salary never attracts employee National Insurance, so the same pension
contribution costs less in take-home pay. Some employers also rebate a share
of their own employer National Insurance saving into the pension pot on top.
The strategy applies to employed users — full-time or part-time — whose
workplace pension contributions are not already made by salary sacrifice; it
has nothing to offer the self-employed, the retired, or anyone without an
employer to sacrifice with.

## Why Fynla quantifies it this way

Fynla computes the saving on the user's actual recorded annual contribution,
not an assumed one, using the employee National Insurance rates held in the
live tax configuration. The rate that applies depends on where the sacrificed
slice of salary falls: the main rate applies below the upper earnings limit,
the lower additional rate applies above it, and where the slice straddles the
limit Fynla blends the two across the exact portions either side. When the
employer's pension scheme records a rebate of employer National Insurance,
Fynla adds that rebate to the total, because it is real money arriving in the
pot. The result is the annual National Insurance saving from switching the
existing contribution — no change in pension amount, no change in take-home
pay assumptions beyond the saving itself.

## Where it sits in sequence

Salary sacrifice complements the other pension strategies rather than
competing with them: it changes how an existing contribution is paid, not
whether new money goes in, so it never fights for the same surplus. It pairs
naturally with the income-band strategies — any extra contribution Fynla
recommends for the taper band or the additional-rate band is cheaper still
when made by sacrifice, because the National Insurance saving stacks on top
of the income tax relief. There is nothing it must wait for and nothing it
conflicts with.

## Claim tier and voicing

Mechanical tier: the saving follows from the recorded contribution and
published National Insurance rates, so Fyn states the working directly — the
contribution, the rate that applies to it, and the annual saving. Voicing
makes clear that the pension contribution itself does not change, only the
route it takes, and names any employer rebate separately so the user can see
which part depends on their employer's policy.
