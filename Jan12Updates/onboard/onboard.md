# Narrative Onboarding Journey for a Holistic Financial Planning Platform

This document describes a **story-driven onboarding experience** designed to make users feel like their financial life is being understood, respected, and gradually revealed — not interrogated. The goal is to replace form-heavy onboarding with a **guided personal journey** that builds trust, motivation, and long-term engagement.

---

## 1. Core Philosophy

The onboarding experience must feel like **self-discovery**, not data entry.

Key principles:
- The user is the protagonist of their financial story.
- Every question must answer: *“Why does this matter to me?”*
- Insight comes before precision.
- Choice builds ownership.
- Progress should feel meaningful, not transactional.

The platform should feel like a **calm financial companion**, not a spreadsheet.

---

## 2. The Five-Act Narrative Structure

The onboarding journey follows a classic story arc to mirror how humans process change and commitment.

### Act I — Identity & Intent
Purpose: Establish relevance and emotional safety  
Emotion: Curiosity  
Outcome: The user feels “this is about me”.

### Act II — Current Reality
Purpose: Capture the present state without judgement  
Emotion: Relief  
Outcome: The user feels understood, not evaluated.

### Act III — Tension & Stakes
Purpose: Show why clarity matters  
Emotion: Motivation  
Outcome: The user sees consequences and opportunity.

### Act IV — Vision of the Future
Purpose: Anchor planning in aspiration  
Emotion: Hope  
Outcome: The user wants to move forward.

### Act V — First Win
Purpose: Deliver immediate value  
Emotion: Confidence  
Outcome: The user trusts the platform.

---

## 3. Identity Without Interrogation

Instead of demographic forms, users are invited to describe **their current chapter in life**.

Examples of identity framing:
- Building stability
- Growing wealth
- Protecting family
- Planning freedom
- Running a business

This approach:
- Feels human and reflective
- Avoids early numerical pressure
- Allows behind-the-scenes persona tagging and defaults

Users should feel *recognised*, not categorised.

---

## 4. Gentle Capture of Current Reality

Information is gathered gradually, one concept at a time, using ranges and feelings instead of precise numbers.

Types of inputs:
- Income ranges
- Household structure
- Financial comfort level
- Primary sources of financial stress

Design intent:
- No judgement
- No forced precision
- Always optional

Progress is framed as **chapters of a story**, not steps of a form.

---

## 5. Early Insight & Reflection

Before asking for detailed data, the platform reflects back what it has learned so far.

This may include:
- Estimated financial health
- Likely strengths
- Likely vulnerabilities

This moment is crucial:
- It builds trust
- It proves the system is listening
- It rewards the user early

Even approximate insights feel powerful when delivered thoughtfully.

---

## 6. The Fork in the Road (Stakes Without Fear)

The user is shown a contrast between:
- Continuing without clarity
- Moving forward with a plan

This is not fear-based.
It is calm, factual, and respectful.

The aim is motivation, not anxiety:
- Show opportunity
- Show optionality
- Avoid scare tactics

---

## 7. User-Led Focus Selection (Not Forced Segmentation)

Rather than asking users to choose a single planning area, the platform invites them to **prioritise what matters most right now**.

Focus areas may include:
- Day-to-day money
- Goals & milestones
- Protection & resilience
- Retirement & later life
- Business growth

Rules:
- Multiple selections allowed
- Order sets emphasis
- Nothing is excluded

The platform remains **holistic**, but the experience adapts to user intent.

---

## 8. Vision Before Data

Before detailed financial inputs, users are invited to imagine their future.

This includes:
- What “secure” means to them
- What they want less of in their life
- What certainty would unlock for them

This reframes planning as:
- A tool for life design
- Not just optimisation

The emotional anchor is hope.

---

## 9. Earning the Right to Ask for Data

Only after context and value are established does the platform request detailed financial information.

Data inputs may include:
- Bank accounts
- Investments
- Pensions
- Debts

Each request is framed as:
- Improving accuracy
- Strengthening forecasts
- Making insights more personal

Skipping is always allowed.

---

## 10. First Meaningful Outcome

Upon completion, the user is presented with:
- Where they are now
- Where they are likely heading
- One clear next action

This is not labelled as a “dashboard”.
It is framed as **their story so far**.

The user should feel:
- More informed
- Less anxious
- Ready to continue

---

## 11. Commitment Without Pressure

The user chooses how the story continues.

Options may include:
- Weekly check-ins
- Monthly reviews
- Life-event-only updates

This reinforces:
- Autonomy
- Respect
- Trust

The platform adapts to the user’s preferred rhythm.

---

## 12. Gamification (Subtle and Adult)

Gamification is used to support progress, not distract from it.

Included:
- Chapter progression
- Milestone recognition
- Progress indicators

Excluded:
- Cartoon rewards
- Loud animations
- Infantilising language

The reward is **clarity and momentum**.

---

## 13. Holistic by Default, Modular by Emphasis

The platform should never ask:
“Do you want budgeting, retirement, or protection?”

Instead:
- Everything feeds a single holistic model
- The UI surfaces what matters most now
- Emphasis changes as life changes

This avoids fragmentation and builds long-term trust.

---

## 14. Why This Approach Works

- Humans remember stories better than numbers
- Early insight builds confidence
- Choice increases commitment
- Progress reduces anxiety
- Agency improves retention

This onboarding makes the user feel:
“I am being understood — and this is helping me.”

---

## 15. What This Enables Long Term

- AI-led check-ins framed as story updates
- Life-event prompts as new chapters
- Natural progression into deeper planning
- A product users emotionally invest in

---

**Outcome:**  
The user does not feel onboarded.  
They feel *seen*, *supported*, and *ready to continue their story*.
