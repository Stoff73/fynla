# Registry — Legal and Regulatory Sources

**Status:** Created 2026-08-21 by `compliance-lead` under `ops/gaps/G-0003`, authorised
by team-lead with a defined scope. **Awaiting CSJ correction**, in the house style of
the other registry files.
**Owner:** CSJ. Amendments gated.
**Maintained by:** `compliance-lead` — `05-perimeter.md` §7.2 requires the sources it
holds to be *"maintained as a dated source register"*. This is that register.

> **Nothing in this file is legal advice, and no agent may treat it as such.**
> It records **what was read, when, and what state it was in.** It does not record what
> any provision means, requires, or implies — that is the determination
> `05-perimeter.md` §7.3 forbids. **A row here is evidence that a citation was checked
> on a date. It is never authority for a conclusion.**

## 1. Why this exists, and the rule it enforces

`05-perimeter.md` §7.2: **"A citation without a date is not a citation."**

Before this file existed, every artefact needing a register built its own inline and it
died with the artefact. Five did (`G-0003`). Nothing accumulated, so **nothing could be
re-checked when the law moved** — and it moved three times without anyone noticing:

| Caught | How | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| PECR reg 6 substituted 5 Feb 2026 (Data (Use and Access) Act 2025) | Someone happened to look | W-0050's citation was stale when written |
| MCA 2005 Sch 1 pending Powers of Attorney Act 2023 amendments | Someone happened to look | Flagged as a trap on W-0100; resolved on W-0102–W-0109 |
| **The lasting power of attorney registration fee moved £82 → £92 on 17 Nov 2025** | Someone happened to look | **Nine months of a wrong number told to users on three surfaces** (W-0109) |

The third is the one that changes the argument. The first two cost accuracy **between
agents**. The third cost **a wrong number to a user about money they pay**.

## 2. Where a source lives predicts how it goes stale — read this before adding a row

**This is the structural finding from W-0109 and it is why §3 is split three ways.**
The three homes behave differently and **watching the wrong one gives false comfort.**

| Class | How it moves | What warns you | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — body text of an Act or regulation** | By amendment to that provision | `legislation.gov.uk` annotates *"changes to legislation"* and *"yet to be applied"* **against the provision itself** | **Highest.** Check the annotations and you have checked |
| **B — a Schedule, or an amount set outside the operative provision** | By a **separate amending order**, leaving the parent regulation **unchanged** | **Nothing, if you are watching the regulation.** The regulation you would naturally cite does not move | **Trap.** Watch the Schedule and the fees orders, never the regulation |
| **C — an undated published web page** | Silently, whenever the publisher edits it | **Nothing at all.** No commencement, no annotation, often no last-updated date | **Lowest.** Datable only as "read on"; **prefer saying less to quoting it** |

**Worked example, class B — the one that cost money.** The lasting power of attorney
registration fee is **not** in regulation 5 of S.I. 2007/2051, which says only that a
fee *"shall be payable"*. **The amount is in the Schedule**, and it moved under
**S.I. 2025/1126 (17 Nov 2025)** without regulation 5 changing a word. An agent citing
"regulation 5" and re-reading it would have found nothing wrong, twice.

**Worked example, class C.** The Office of the Public Guardian's published registration
timescale carries **no last-updated date on the page**. That is why W-0109 ruled the fee
**in** (class A/B, statutory, dated) and the timescale **out** (class C) — same item,
different homes, opposite answers.

## 4. Standing warnings

**W1 — Mental Capacity Act 2005, Schedule 1: the registration scheme is mid-reform.**
Pending **Powers of Attorney Act 2023 (2023 c. 42)** amendments are outstanding against
Sch 1 paras **4(1)(a), 4(2), 4(3), 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13 and 14** — that is the
**registration** machinery. **Paras 1 and 2 are clear**, which is why W-0102–W-0108 could
proceed. **Anyone building against registration must check commencement first**, and the
2023 Act's own commencement section **has not been read** — the status above is read from
the annotations against the amended provisions, not from the amending Act.

**W2 — Wills Act 1837 s.9(2): a closed window, and a live lesson.** Video witnessing was
permitted *"in relation to wills made on or after 31 January 2020 and on or before
31 January 2024"*. **That window has closed.** So *"in accordance with the Wills Act
1837"* meant something different for a will made in 2022 than for one made today, and
**an undated citation cannot express that.** Kept as the clearest worked example of why
§7.2's rule is not pedantry.

**W3 — two regime rows in `05-perimeter.md` §1.1 still have no rows here.** The DMCC Act
2024 Part 4 (commencement of Chapter 2 **could not be pinned at all**) and the ICO's
special-category guidance (§6.5). **Their absence is scope, not clearance** — see §5.
*(PECR was the third and now has rows A11–A12, added 2026-08-21.)*

**W4 — PECR reg 6 was replaced, not amended, and a citation to it may not resolve at
all.** This is the sharpest instance of the whole register's point, so it is recorded in
full.

**Reg 6 as in force today reads, in its entirety:**

> *"6.—(1) Subject to Schedule A1, a person must not store information, or gain access to
> information stored, in the terminal equipment of a subscriber or user. (2) In paragraph
> (1) and Schedule A1— (a) a reference (however expressed) to storing information, or
> gaining access to information stored, in the terminal equipment of a subscriber or user
> includes a reference to instigating the storage or access, and (b) except as otherwise
> provided, a reference (however expressed) to gaining access to information stored in the
> terminal equipment of a subscriber or user includes a reference to collecting or
> monitoring information automatically emitted by the terminal equipment."*

**That is the whole regulation. It has two paragraphs.** Every exception — including
consent — now lives in **Schedule A1**, which **did not exist before 5 February 2026**.

**Consequence, and it is not the ordinary staleness this register was built for.**
`ops/reports/2026-08-21-W-0050-consent-validity-ruling.md` cites **reg 6(4)**. Reg 6 as in
force **has no paragraph (4)**. So that is not a citation that has drifted in meaning —
**it is a citation that no longer resolves against the provision it names.** A reader
checking it finds nothing, rather than finding something subtly different.

**This is the second time in one day a PECR citation has failed**, and the two failed
differently. The register exists because the first one was caught by luck.

**Do not treat this as reopening W-0050**, whose substance is parked by CSJ. Recording
that a citation no longer resolves is register maintenance, not a re-ruling.

**W5 — Schedule A1 paragraph numbers: enumerate, never infer. Row A12 carries all seven.**

**This register caught its first error on the day it was built, and the error was in a
register.** `ops/reports/2026-08-21-W-0050-consent-validity-ruling.md` recorded Schedule A1
as *"para 1 consent · para 3 strictly necessary · para 4 statistical purposes"*. **Every
number is off by one**, because **paragraph 1 is Interpretation** and the exceptions start
at 2. Corrected there on 2026-08-21 with markers at both the register and §0; **the
substance that document described was right and none of its reasoning was re-opened.**

**How it was caught, and it was not by anything systematic:** a second agent read Schedule
A1 independently for a different item (W-0049 / F-0007) and the two enumerations disagreed.
**Nothing would have surfaced it otherwise** — the numbers were internally consistent, cited
confidently, and written the same day from a source read the same day.

**Two lessons, both load-bearing for how this file is used:**

1. **A wrong register is worse than no register, because it is trusted.** The whole value of
   row A12 is that it is the one copy; that is also what makes an error in it expensive.
   **Anyone relying on a row must be willing to be the one who re-reads it.**
2. **Enumerate the whole instrument, not the paragraphs you need.** The error came from
   recording three exceptions by name without listing what sat above them. Row A12 lists all
   seven paragraphs for exactly that reason, including the four nobody currently relies on.

## 6. Adding a row

1. **Read the source directly.** Never from memory, never from a search summary
   (`05-perimeter.md` §7.2).
2. **Classify it A, B or C** using §2. If B, record **where the value actually lives** and
   **what to watch**, which is usually not the provision you would cite.
3. Record the **version read** and the **date checked** — both, always.
4. Record the **amendment status as displayed**, including outstanding effects, and say
   whether you read commencement from the amending instrument or from the annotations.
5. Name the **work item** that relied on it, so a moved source can be traced to what it
   affected.
6. **A rejected source gets a row too**, with the reason. See C2.
