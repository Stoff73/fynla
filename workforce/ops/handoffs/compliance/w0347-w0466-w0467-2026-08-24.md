## 2. W-0347 — FLAGGED

The fix does what its commit claims. Verified directly:
`SpouseLinkingService::linkExistingSpouse()` writes nothing to the other account
(`app/Services/Onboarding/SpouseLinkingService.php:245-290`); `establishAcceptedLink()`
is the only both-row writer and is reachable only from `accept()` (`:315-365`);
`revoke()` marks `rejected` rather than deleting
(`app/Http/Controllers/Api/SpousePermissionController.php:341-352`); the response
carries `{id, name, email}` instead of the model (`:41-56`). **Article 7(3)'s withdrawal
limb has a working mechanism behind it, on web and `/m`.**

### F1 — the migration's stated safeguard is inverted. It excludes nothing the defect created.

`database/migrations/2026_08_23_120000_backfill_spouse_permissions_for_existing_links.php:19-21`:

> "Scope is deliberately narrow: RECIPROCAL links only. A one-sided link is precisely
> what the old flow could forge, and granting it an accepted row here would launder
> exactly the defect being fixed."

**The old flow could not produce a one-sided link at all.** From the code removed in
`70c5014da`:

```php
-            $currentUser->spouse_id = $lockedSpouse->id;
-            $lockedSpouse->spouse_id = $currentUser->id;
```

Both sides, always. Every link the forging path created was reciprocal by construction,
so "reciprocal only" admits precisely and only what the defect produced. **Re-verified
after `26564407f`: the migration is unchanged.**

Whatever is decided about the rows, **the docblock must not assert a screen it does not
perform.** An auditor reading it will conclude the population was filtered.

### F2 — the forged rows were never touched, and the UI still tells the victim they accepted

Two populations, and the question conflates them:

| Population | Fingerprint | Written by | What the fix did |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — forged** | `requested_at IS NULL AND responded_at IS NOT NULL` | old `createSpousePermissions()` (set only `responded_at`) | **nothing** — `insertOrIgnore` no-ops on an existing row |
| **B — inherited** | **both** timestamps NULL | the new backfill | created them |

Measured on the local development database (`laravel`), 13 rows:

- **10 population-A rows — all test accounts.** 6 seeded preview personas
  (`is_preview_user = 1`: young_family, peak_earners, retired_couple); 4 belonging to
  `sarah.jones@example.com` / `david.jones@example.com` and `pt.throwaway.*+0821@example.com`.
- **2 population-B rows** — `john@example.com` / `jane@example.com`, seeded test users.
- **1 genuine `rejected` row**, both timestamps set, from the browser verification.

The census acceptance 3 asks for is one query:

```sql
SELECT user_id, spouse_id, responded_at FROM spouse_permissions
WHERE requested_at IS NULL AND responded_at IS NOT NULL;
```

**What population A does today:** `User::hasAcceptedSpousePermission()`
(`app/Models/User.php:830-832`) returns the row's status verbatim, so sharing stays on.
And because `responded_at` is populated,
`resources/js/components/UserProfile/SpouseDataSharing.vue:154-161` renders
**"Data Sharing Enabled — Accepted: 20 August 2026"** for a consent nobody gave, with a
plausible date attached. **The screen asserts the false thing at the exact point the
state is shown** — worse than the silent gap it replaced.

**Applying `05-perimeter.md` §4** (ratified: *where Fynla knows its picture is
incomplete, it says so at the point the affected figure is shown*): §4 is **not met**.
Closing it is the conservative action and may moot the legal question — the `Q-15`
shape.

**Replacement, for any row with `requested_at IS NULL`:**

> "This sharing was already switched on when we introduced confirmations, so we do not
> have a record of it being agreed. You can stop sharing at any time."

### F3 — the consent notice understates what acceptance does, and the extra part is irreversible

Both surfaces describe acceptance as data viewing only:

- web `SpouseDataSharing.vue:114-116` — "This will allow them to see your assets,
  liabilities, and income in their account view."
- `/m` `resources/mobile/views/SpouseSharing.vue:26-28` — "Accepting lets them see your
  assets, liabilities and income."

`establishAcceptedLink()` (`:348-357`) also writes, on the **accepter's own row**:
`spouse_id`, an **overwritten `marital_status`**, and a **changed `household_id`**.
None is stated at the point of consent. `revoke()` reverses none of it — defensibly
("revoking withdraws visibility, not the marriage") — but the board records that
**no unlink exists on any surface.** One click does a disclosed reversible thing and an
undisclosed permanent one.

**Also: the grant is mutual and is disclosed as one-way.** One accepted row makes
`hasAcceptedSpousePermission()` true for both parties. Every pre-consent screen on both
surfaces describes it one-directionally; only the post-consent screens say "each see the
other's". **Mutuality is disclosed after the decision, not before it.**

**Replacement — `/m` incoming request** (`SpouseSharing.vue:26-28`):

> "**{Name}** has asked to link their Fynla household with yours. If you accept, you will
> each be able to see the other's assets, liabilities and income, and your accounts will
> be recorded as one household. You can stop sharing at any time. Nothing is shared until
> you accept."

**Replacement — web equivalent** (`SpouseDataSharing.vue:114-116`):

> "Your spouse has requested permission to view your financial data. If you accept, you
> will each be able to see the other's assets, liabilities and income, and your accounts
> will be recorded as one household. You can stop sharing at any time."

### F4 — withdrawal is a one-way door

`SpousePermissionController::request()` (`:190-206`) refuses with 422 if **any** row
exists, whatever its status. After revoke or decline the row exists as `rejected`.
`linkExistingSpouse()` returns `already_linked: true` for a linked pair without creating
a new invitation (`:216-231`). The web `isRejected` branch renders no button
(`SpouseDataSharing.vue:175-201`); the `/m` `sharing-off` section renders none
(`SpouseSharing.vue:68-74`).

**Once sharing is off, neither party can turn it back on through any interface.**
A withdrawal a user cannot reverse is one they will hesitate to make. PRIN 2A.6
(internal standard) points at it. **Functional defect as much as a compliance one.**

### F5 — contradictory permission records

The backfill's join yields **both** directions per couple and the unique key permits
both — confirmed in the data. `revoke()` and `hasAcceptedSpousePermission()` both use
`->first()` on an `orWhere` with **no `orderBy`**. They will usually pick the same row;
nothing guarantees it, and the two queries are not textually identical. **If they
diverge, the user withdraws and sharing stays on** — the exact failure the
`rejected`-not-deleted change exists to prevent. After a revoke the database holds one
`accepted` and one `rejected` row for the same couple; **Art. 7(1) requires the
controller to be able to demonstrate consent**, and a self-contradictory pair is a poor
thing to demonstrate from.

`26564407f` notes acceptance now writes **one** row, which fixes this forward. **The
backfill still writes two, and the historic rows are still two.** Add `->orderBy('id')`
to both reads, or reduce to one row per couple.

Noted, not flagged: `createSpousePermissionsForCreatedAccount()` was **removed** by
`26564407f`, which retires the auto-accept path entirely. Good — it also removes the
concern that future auto-accepts would be shape-identical to genuine ones.

### The headline question, answered

**"Is retrospectively legitimising forged consent acceptable, or must those households
be re-asked?"**

1. **On dev, nothing needs to happen — and the reason is not that dev is staging.** It is
   that **every one of the 10 forged rows belongs to a test account**: 6 seeded preview
   personas, 4 `@example.com`/throwaway. **There is no data subject to re-ask.**
   Reseeding replaces them. **Yes, that changes the answer, and it changes it for a
   different reason than the one offered.**
2. **Dev therefore cannot settle it, and it is being asked in the wrong place.** The
   migration ships to production at release. **Production has never been measured.** Run
   the F2 query against `fynla.org` **before the release PR**. Note that production has
   two populations needing two decisions — A (forged, sharing live, "Accepted: <date>"
   on screen) and B (no row at all, provenance unknown and **not** attributable to this
   defect, because the forging path always wrote rows).
3. **Whether an inherited row may stand in place of consent is `Q-17` and needs a
   lawyer.** Not answered here and no other agent should answer it either.
4. **What needs no lawyer, and is what I would act on today:** stop the UI asserting an
   acceptance that did not happen (F2), correct the migration docblock (F1), give people
   a way back in (F4). Conservative, cheap, and they may make Q-17 academic — which is
   §7.4's whole purpose.

### F — the single branch is not the single/unmarried branch

The board records CSJ's decision as: *"a single or unmarried user keeps 'your estate
could be subject to up to £X', because for them the figure genuinely is their own estate
on their own death."* **The premise is right. The branch does not implement it.**

The predicate is `($calculation['is_married'] ?? false) && ($calculation['data_sharing_enabled'] ?? false)`
(`EstateIhtExposureDetector.php:73`), and `$isMarried` itself requires a linked spouse

catches three groups:

1. genuinely single or unmarried users — correct for them;
2. **married users whose spouse has no linked Fynla account;**
3. **married users with sharing off or revoked.**

For 2 and 3 it says "**Your estate** could be subject to up to £X" to a married person
whose own first-death liability, with the spouse exemption, is typically £0. **That is

**The figure is right** — W-0154 F3 correctly moved the allowance doubling onto

> "Based on your own records alone, your estate could be subject to up to £X in
> Inheritance Tax. This figure does not allow for anything passing to your partner.
> Linking your accounts gives a fuller picture."

Genuinely single and unmarried users keep the existing sentence unchanged.

**The real inconsistency is F: the branch boundary is not where CSJ was told it was.**

### G — the email offers a means of refusal that cannot function

It includes `emails.modules.footer` with `variant => 'dark'`, which renders the default
link set including **"Unsubscribe" → `https://fynla.org/unsubscribe`**.

**That route does not exist.** The only unsubscribe route is
`/unsubscribe/news/{token}` (`routes/web.php:66-67`, newsletter, token-bound). The link
falls through to the SPA catch-all.

**And even if it resolved, it could not be honoured** — W-0472 establishes the invited
address is used once and dropped, so there is no record to suppress. **A recipient who
wants to refuse has no working way to, and Fynla has nowhere to record it if they did.**

**Whether a refusal mechanism is legally required here is not mine to determine.** PECR
reg 22(2) governs unsolicited electronic mail "for the purposes of direct marketing" to
individual subscribers; whether this email is direct marketing is precisely the kind of
characterisation §7.3 forbids me, and **PECR is `Unmapped` in the regime map** (§1.1),
so per §1.3 this finding is **provisional on its face** and is written as `Q-19` in §7.

**What is not a legal question:** the email presents an unsubscribe link, the link is
broken, and the suppression it implies is impossible. **Either make it work or remove
it** — an inoperative refusal mechanism is worse than none, because it looks like a
control and is not.

**Minimum change if the link is removed:** pass an explicit `$links` array to the footer
without the unsubscribe entry, and add to the "What happens next" panel:

> "We will not email this address again unless you create an account."

That is only true if it is true — see §6.

### H — no perimeter line on an acquisition email

The email states "Fynla is a United Kingdom financial planning app" and its call to
action is "Create your account". **The claim made is accurate and modest — it does not
claim to give advice and does not claim to be regulated, so this is not a hard block.**
But it is a public claim about what Fynla does, sent unsolicited to a prospect, with no
statement of regulatory posture. `05-perimeter.md` §3 binds it.

**Suggested addition to the body paragraphs:**

> "Fynla provides guidance to help you understand your own finances. It is not a
> regulated financial adviser and does not give financial advice."

### Noted, not flagged

The inviter's display name is user-controlled and is delivered to an address of the
inviter's choosing. Escaping stops markup injection; it does not stop someone setting
their name to a sentence and using Fynla to deliver it. Mitigated only by the 5/hour
throttle. **Not a compliance block — raised for `security-reviewer`.**

## 6. W-0472 — the invited address is not retained

**Position, as a reviewer and not as a block.**

The board frames retention as a product convenience — see who you invited, fix a typo,
re-send. **That framing is incomplete, and G is why.** Retention is also what makes a
refusal recordable. Without any record:

- a recipient asking never to be contacted again cannot be honoured, because there is
  nothing to write the suppression against;
- **a second invitation to the same address is indistinguishable from the first**, so
  "we will not contact you again" cannot be promised truthfully, and repeat invitations
  cannot be prevented;
- the broken unsubscribe link in G cannot be fixed by fixing the route alone.

**So the choice is not "retain for convenience or don't".** It is:

| Option | What it costs | What it buys |
|---|---|---|
| **Retain the address** | holding a non-user's personal data, needing a lawful basis, a retention period, and a purge — none of which exist today | the screen can name the invitee, a typo is recoverable, **a refusal can be recorded and honoured**, repeats can be suppressed |
| **Retain nothing** | no record, so **no suppression is possible** and the unsubscribe link must be removed rather than fixed | nothing held, nothing to purge, smallest footprint |
| **Retain a one-way hash only** | cannot show the invitee the address, so W-0472's product complaint is unsolved | a repeat and a refusal can both be matched without storing the address in the clear |

**The third is worth putting to CSJ and is not in the board item.** It separates the
product question (show the address) from the compliance one (recognise the address
again), and only the product half requires holding it legibly.

**What I will not do is decide it.** Storing a non-user's personal data needs a lawful
basis, and identifying one is a determination §7.3 forbids me. **Whichever way it goes,
the retention period and the purge path must be decided at the same time** — `Q-16`
already records a live instance of exactly this shape (anonymous consent rows that no
retention path reaches), and this would be the second.

**If retention is chosen, note also:** any re-send must not become the enumeration
