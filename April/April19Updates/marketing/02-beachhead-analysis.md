## Segment 1 — IHT-Anxious Mass-Affluent Couples

### 1. Burning Pain — 8/10
- IHT thresholds frozen through 2029/30 (Nov 2024 Budget extension) → fiscal-drag fuse is lit; more households cross into IHT every year
- HMRC IHT receipts hit a record £7.5bn in 2023/24, up ~15% YoY; forecast >£9bn by 2028
- ONS: ~4% of estates pay IHT today; Office for Budget Responsibility projects 6–7% by 2030 as house-price inflation drags mass-affluent across the frozen NRB
- **Emotional urgency is high** ("we worked for this, not HMRC"), but the pain is *periodic* (annual gifting cycles, birthdays, Budget days) rather than daily. Consequence is *catastrophic but silent* — mistakes only surface at probate.
- Workarounds: spreadsheets, one-off STEP plan at £2k–£5k, occasional IFA meeting

**Why not 9–10**: Deferred pain. You don't wake up angry about IHT on a Tuesday; you think about it when the Telegraph runs a piece.

### 2. Willingness to Pay — 9/10
- Already spending £2k–£5k on one-off estate planning, £8k–£20k/yr on IFA AUM fees (1% of £800k–£2m)
- £25–£50/month is noise vs a potential £100k–£400k IHT saving — the ROI math is the clearest in consumer fintech
- Decision is usually one spouse driving, the other assenting — low complexity
- Retirees have time + discipline to actually *use* the product

### 3. Winnability — 5/10
- TAM ~1.2m UK households (ONS Wealth & Assets Survey, top decile with private pensions)
- SAM (digitally literate, unadvised, actively IHT-anxious) ~300k
- **SOM in 18 months: realistically 2–5%, not Moore's 60–70%.** This is the weak spot.
- **Distribution is hard**: this cohort doesn't live on TikTok/LinkedIn. Reached via *Telegraph Money*, *Money Box* (BBC R4), Saga, Rest Less, Which?, paraplanner/IFA referral
- **Trust gap**: 60–70 year olds are cautious about new fintechs holding their estate data
- Incumbents (SJP, Brewin, Evelyn, HL) are complacent but have heavy brand weight

### 4. Referral Potential — 7/10
- Golf-club / dinner-party word-of-mouth is strong but slow
- **Cross-generational loop**: adult children (25–45) referring parents (and vice versa) is structurally possible because Fynla's household model already links across generations via gifting flows
- Affiliate channel via solicitors, STEP members, will-writers, accountants
- Not viral — but very sticky once trust is earned

## Segment 2 — UK Owner-Operator Limited Company Directors

### 1. Burning Pain — 9/10
- Tax pain is *constant*: monthly payroll, quarterly VAT, annual CT600, year-end dividend vs pension decisions
- **Recent policy shocks** have made this white-hot:
  - Corp tax rose to 25% (Apr 2023)
  - Dividend tax bands hit 8.75 / 33.75 / 39.35%
  - **Oct 2024 Budget capped BPR at £1m combined** — founders who assumed their business passed IHT-free now face potential £400k+ exposure on a £2m business. *This is the clearest market-timing wedge available right now.*
  - IR35 continues to harass contractors since 2021
- Integration pain: Xero/FreeAgent handle the business, pensions sit on HL/PensionBee, personal sits in spreadsheets, estate sits nowhere — founder is the integrator
- Key-person cover is frequently mis-sized or missing — silent risk they know they have

### 2. Willingness to Pay — 10/10
- Already spending £1.5k–£5k/yr accountant + £500–£2k insurance + often £1k–£3k IFA
- £50–£100/month feels cheap vs the adjacency of those costs
- **Tax-deductible through the company** — the business becomes the payer, not the founder's disposable income
- Founder-director = total budget autonomy, no committee
- Founders are subscription-native (already pay for Notion, Superhuman, Xero, Slack, etc.)

### 3. Winnability — 8/10
- TAM ~500k UK owner-operator Ltd Cos with >£100k revenue (Companies House + HMRC data)
- SAM ~150k digitally-literate, self-service-inclined directors active on LinkedIn/IndieHackers/ContractorUK
- **White space is genuinely clear**: no integrated product exists that models *personal wealth + Ltd Co extraction + BPR + estate + key-person cover* on one dashboard
- Reachable via LinkedIn, founder Twitter/X, IndieHackers, r/UKPersonalFinance, ContractorUK (200k+ members), IPSE, FSB
- 60–70% of SAM in 18 months is still aggressive, but **15–25% of a tight sub-niche** (e.g. £100k–£300k revenue IT contractors, or agency owners in London) is realistic — which is what Moore actually requires

### 4. Referral Potential — 9/10
- Founder-to-founder referral culture is one of the strongest in any market
- Accountants are a warm referral channel (they want clients to be more organised, not less)
- Professional communities: IPSE, FSB, IndieHackers, UK Tech Twitter, ContractorUK
- **Natural expansion path**: today's 45-year-old Ltd Co director becomes tomorrow's Segment 1 IHT-anxious retiree — the product keeps the customer for 30 years
- Also expands laterally: director → spouse → children → household estate (direct onramp to Segment 1)

## Recommendation

**Launch on Segment 2 (Ltd Co Directors). Expand into Segment 1 (IHT-anxious couples) only after 15%+ SOM and 25+ named reference customers in Segment 2.**

Three reasons this ordering beats the reverse:

1. **Market timing wedge**: the Oct 2024 BPR cap is a one-time acquisition catalyst. Every UK founder with a business valued above £1m has a concrete reason to re-plan *right now*. That wedge closes as the market adapts.
2. **Moore "bowling alley" logic**: Segment 2 validates the estate + household + business-valuation stack under easier-to-reach customers, producing references that Segment 1 (trust-sensitive, word-of-mouth driven) will actually accept. The reverse direction doesn't work — retirees don't refer founders.
3. **Cash conversion**: founder-led B2C2B with the company as payer converts 3–5× faster than mass-affluent consumer acquisition through editorial channels.
