# UK Tax Year 2026/27 — What's Changed

**Tax Year:** 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027
**Prepared:** 3 April 2026

This document summarises the key tax rates, allowances, and thresholds for the 2026/27 tax year, highlighting what has changed from last year.

---

## At a Glance — Key Changes

| Area | What Changed | 2025/26 | 2026/27 |
|------|-------------|---------|---------|
| Dividend tax (basic rate) | Up 2 percentage points | 8.75% | **10.75%** |
| Dividend tax (higher rate) | Up 2 percentage points | 33.75% | **35.75%** |
| Business Asset Disposal Relief | Up 4 percentage points | 14% | **18%** |
| Farm and business inheritance relief | New cap introduced | 100% unlimited | **100% on first £2.5m, 50% above** |
| AIM shares inheritance relief | Halved | 100% | **50%** |
| State Pension (full, new) | Up 4.8% | £230.25/week | **£241.30/week** |
| National Living Wage (21+) | Up 4.1% | £12.21/hour | **£12.71/hour** |
| Child Benefit (eldest child) | Up 3.8% | £26.05/week | **£27.05/week** |
| Statutory Sick Pay | Increased | £118.75/week | **£123.25/week** |
| Premium Bonds prize rate | Reduced | 3.6% | **3.3%** |
| Universal Credit (limited capability, new claims) | Halved | £416.19/month | **£217.26/month** |
| Two-child benefit limit | Removed | Limited to 2 children | **All children now qualify** |

## Income Tax

The main income tax rates and thresholds are **unchanged** — they remain frozen until at least April 2031.

| Band | Taxable Income | Rate |
|------|---------------|------|
| Personal Allowance | Up to £12,570 | 0% |
| Basic Rate | £12,571 to £50,270 | 20% |
| Higher Rate | £50,271 to £125,140 | 40% |
| Additional Rate | Over £125,140 | 45% |

- Your Personal Allowance reduces by £1 for every £2 you earn above £100,000 and is fully lost at £125,140.
- The Personal Savings Allowance remains £1,000 (basic rate), £500 (higher rate), and nil (additional rate).
- The starting rate for savings income is still £5,000 at 0%.

---

## National Insurance

All National Insurance rates and thresholds are **unchanged**.

**Employees (Class 1)**
- You pay 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 per year
- You pay 2% on earnings above £50,270

**Employers**
- Employers pay 15% on earnings above £5,000 per year

**Self-Employed (Class 4)**
- You pay 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270
- You pay 2% on profits above £50,270

Class 2 National Insurance contributions remain effectively abolished for most people.

## Dividends

**Changed this year.** Dividend tax rates have increased by 2 percentage points at the basic and higher rate levels.

| Band | 2025/26 | 2026/27 |
|------|---------|---------|
| Tax-free allowance | £500 | £500 |
| Basic rate | 8.75% | **10.75%** |
| Higher rate | 33.75% | **35.75%** |
| Additional rate | 39.35% | 39.35% |

If you receive dividend income (for example from shares or a business you own), you will pay more tax on it this year.

---

## Capital Gains Tax

Most Capital Gains Tax rates are **unchanged**.

| Type of Gain | Basic Rate | Higher Rate |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Shares and other assets | 18% | 24% |
| Residential property | 18% | 24% |
| Annual tax-free amount | £3,000 | £3,000 |

### Business Asset Disposal Relief — Changed

If you sell all or part of a qualifying business, the tax rate on the gain has increased:

- **Was:** 14%
- **Now:** 18%
- Lifetime limit remains £1,000,000
- You must have owned the business for at least 2 years

## ISAs (Individual Savings Accounts)

**Unchanged.** The annual ISA allowance remains:

| ISA Type | Annual Allowance |
|----------|-----------------|
| Overall ISA limit | £20,000 |
| Lifetime ISA | £4,000 (within the £20,000) |
| Junior ISA | £9,000 |

Note: A new £12,000 annual cap on cash ISA contributions (for under 65s) has been announced, but this does not take effect until April 2027.

## Pensions

Most pension allowances are **unchanged**.

| Allowance | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Annual Allowance | £60,000 |
| Money Purchase Annual Allowance | £10,000 |
| Carry Forward | Up to 3 previous tax years |
| Lifetime Allowance | Abolished |

**Tapered Annual Allowance** (for high earners):
- Starts reducing if your threshold income exceeds £200,000 and adjusted income exceeds £260,000
- Minimum Annual Allowance: £10,000

### State Pension — Changed

The full new State Pension has increased by 4.8%:

| | 2025/26 | 2026/27 |
|---|---------|---------|
| Weekly amount | £230.25 | **£241.30** |
| Annual amount | £11,973 | **£12,547.60** |

- You need 35 qualifying years of National Insurance for the full amount
- Minimum 10 qualifying years to receive anything
- Current State Pension age: 66 (rising to 67 between 2026 and 2028; further rise to 68 planned between 2044 and 2046)

### Minimum Wage — Changed

| Age Group | 2025/26 | 2026/27 |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| 21 and over (National Living Wage) | £12.21/hour | **£12.71/hour** |
| 18 to 20 | £10.00/hour | **£10.85/hour** |
| Under 18 | £7.55/hour | **£8.00/hour** |
| Apprentice | £7.55/hour | **£8.00/hour** |

### Workplace Pensions (Auto-Enrolment)

All auto-enrolment thresholds are **unchanged**:

- Earnings trigger: £10,000 per year
- Qualifying earnings band: £6,240 to £50,270
- Minimum contributions: 8% total (3% employer, 5% employee)

## Inheritance Tax

The core inheritance tax thresholds remain **frozen** (until at least April 2031).

| Threshold | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Nil-Rate Band | £325,000 |
| Residence Nil-Rate Band | £175,000 |
| Combined (individual) | £500,000 |
| Combined (married couple/civil partners) | Up to £1,000,000 |
| Tax rate | 40% (or 36% if 10%+ left to charity) |

The Residence Nil-Rate Band begins to taper if your estate exceeds £2,000,000.

### Farm and Business Relief — Major Change

From April 2026, Agricultural Property Relief and Business Property Relief are reformed:

- **First £2.5 million** of qualifying agricultural or business property: **100% relief** (no inheritance tax)
- **Above £2.5 million**: **50% relief** (effectively taxed at 20% instead of 40%)
- The £2.5 million is a **combined cap** shared between agricultural and business property
- **Transferable between spouses** — a married couple or civil partners can effectively have a £5 million combined cap

### AIM Shares — Changed

Shares listed on the Alternative Investment Market (AIM) previously qualified for 100% Business Property Relief. From April 2026:

- AIM shares now receive only **50% relief** (effectively taxed at 20%)
- AIM shares are treated separately from the £2.5 million cap

### Pensions and Inheritance Tax — Not Yet

The inclusion of unused pension funds in your estate for inheritance tax has been announced but **does not take effect until April 2027**. For this tax year, pensions remain outside your estate.

## Stamp Duty Land Tax

Stamp Duty rates are **unchanged**. The current rates (which took effect 1 April 2025) continue:

**Standard Residential Rates:**

| Property Value | Rate |
|---------------|------|
| Up to £125,000 | 0% |
| £125,001 to £250,000 | 2% |
| £250,001 to £925,000 | 5% |
| £925,001 to £1,500,000 | 10% |
| Over £1,500,000 | 12% |

**First-Time Buyers:** No Stamp Duty on the first £300,000 (for properties up to £500,000), then 5% above £300,000.

**Additional Properties:** 5% surcharge on top of standard rates.

**Non-UK Residents:** Additional 2% surcharge.

## Child and Family Benefits

### Child Benefit — Changed

| | 2025/26 | 2026/27 |
|---|---------|---------|
| Eldest child (weekly) | £26.05 | **£27.05** |
| Each additional child (weekly) | £17.25 | **£17.90** |
| Eldest child (annual) | £1,354.60 | **£1,406.60** |
| Each additional child (annual) | £897.00 | **£930.80** |

The High Income Child Benefit Charge thresholds are unchanged:
- Charge starts at £60,000 (higher earner's income)
- Full clawback at £80,000

**Important:** The **two-child limit** on the child element of Universal Credit and tax credits has been **removed** from April 2026. All children now qualify for the child element.

**Reminder:** Even if you opt out of receiving Child Benefit payments due to high income, you should still submit the claim form. The parent who stays at home receives National Insurance credits towards their State Pension.

### Tax-Free Childcare

Unchanged at up to £2,000 per child per year (£4,000 for disabled children). The government tops up 25p for every £1 you pay in, up to the limit. Available for children up to age 11 (16 if disabled).

Minimum earnings threshold updated to £203.36 per week (reflecting the new National Living Wage of £12.71 per hour for 16 hours).

### Free Childcare Hours

| Age | Hours Per Week | Who Qualifies |
|-----|---------------|---------------|
| 3 to 4 years | 15 hours | All families |
| 3 to 4 years | 30 hours | Working parents |
| 2 years | 15 hours | Working parents |
| 9 months to 2 years | 30 hours | Working parents |
| 2 years (disadvantaged) | 15 hours | Low-income families |

All hours are for 38 weeks per year (term time). Working parents must each earn at least £203.36 per week and no more than £100,000 per year.

## Other Benefits

### Statutory Sick Pay — Changed

| | 2025/26 | 2026/27 |
|---|---------|---------|
| Weekly rate | £118.75 | **£123.25** |
| Maximum weeks | 28 | 28 |

From April 2026, the Lower Earnings Limit for Statutory Sick Pay has been abolished — all eligible employees now qualify regardless of earnings. The three waiting days have also been abolished, meaning Statutory Sick Pay is now payable from day one of sickness absence. For lower earners, the payment is the lower of the flat rate (£123.25 per week) or 80% of normal weekly earnings.

### Universal Credit — Changed

**Standard Allowance (monthly):**

| Circumstance | 2025/26 | 2026/27 |
|-------------|---------|---------|
| Single, under 25 | £316.98 | **£338.58** |
| Single, 25 or over | £400.14 | **£424.90** |
| Couple, both under 25 | £497.55 | **£528.34** |
| Couple, one or both 25+ | £628.15 | **£666.97** |

**Key change:** The Limited Capability for Work and Work-Related Activity element has been **halved for new claims** from April 2026 — down to £217.26 per month (from £416.19). Existing claimants keep the higher rate of £423.27.

### Personal Independence Payment — Changed

| Component | 2025/26 | 2026/27 |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Daily Living (standard) | £73.90/week | **£76.70/week** |
| Daily Living (enhanced) | £110.40/week | **£114.60/week** |
| Mobility (standard) | £29.20/week | **£30.30/week** |
| Mobility (enhanced) | £77.05/week | **£80.00/week** |

### Employment and Support Allowance — Changed

| Rate | 2025/26 | 2026/27 |
|------|---------|---------|
| Assessment rate (under 25) | £71.70/week | **£75.65/week** |
| Assessment rate (25+) | £90.50/week | **£95.55/week** |

### Bereavement Support Payment

Unchanged:
- Higher rate: £3,500 lump sum + £350/month for 18 months
- Lower rate: £2,500 lump sum + £100/month for 18 months

## Trusts

Most trust tax rates are unchanged, except that trust dividend rates have been updated to reflect the new ordinary dividend rate:

- Interest in Possession trust dividend rate: **10.75%** (was 8.75%)
- Trust management expenses dividend relief: **10.75%** (was 8.75%)

Note: From April 2027, trust income tax rates will change significantly — discretionary trusts will pay 47% (currently 45%) and interest in possession trusts will pay 22% (currently 20%).

| Wedding (from parent) | £5,000 |

## Looking Ahead — Changes Coming in 2027/28

These do **not** apply this year, but have already been announced for April 2027:

- Cash ISA annual cap of £12,000 for under 65s
- Savings and property income taxed at higher rates (basic 22%, higher 42%, additional 47%)
- Unused pension funds included in estate for inheritance tax
- Trust tax rates increasing
